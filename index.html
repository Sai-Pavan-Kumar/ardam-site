How a delivery app survives failure

Event sourcing stores every change as an immutable log, so a service can rebuild its state after a crash. When one order spans several services, the saga pattern undoes the finished steps if a later step fails.

    Gemini, OpenAI, Anthropic or any OpenAI-compatible provider.

    Docs, blogs, system design posts: anything you can read.

Fair questions

Does it teach English?

No. It helps you read the article in Tenglish.

What is Tenglish?

Telugu written in English letters, mixed with everyday English words. It's how many of us already text.

Is it word-for-word translation?

No. Ardam rewrites sentences so they sound natural, while its rules tell the AI not to add or remove facts.

Can the AI get something wrong?

Yes. AI can make mistakes, so check anything important against the original. It's one click away.

Why do I need my own key?

Ardam has no server, so you choose the provider and only you pay them, if at all. Some providers offer free limits.

Where does my key go?

It stays in your browser and is sent only to the provider you picked. It isn't encrypted on your device, so avoid shared computers.

What does the provider see?

The text of the page you convert. Don't convert private pages unless you're fine with your provider seeing them.

Does Ardam cost money?

The extension is free. Long pages use more of your provider's limit.

Does it work on every site?

Most article pages should work, but sites are built differently, so some may convert only part of the page or look a little off.

Does it work on PDFs?

Not yet. Web pages come first.

What about other languages?

Tenglish first, so we can get it right. Others may follow.

Can I install it today?

Not yet. It's being tested before it goes to the Chrome Web Store.
